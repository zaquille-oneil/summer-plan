The Night Strategy

You will be high and you will eat. The plan accounts for this — it doesn't fight it.

  • Front-load calories during the day. Eat real meals before 7pm. Target ~1,400 cal by dinner. This leaves ~700 cal for nighttime — enough to satisfy munchies without blowing the budget.
  • Delete DoorDash on day one. This is the single highest-impact rule. Two delivery orders can easily be 2,000+ cal. The friction of not having the app is the point.
  • Stock the pantry, not the kitchen. Have these ready for when you're high: air-popped popcorn, beef/turkey jerky, protein bars, Greek yogurt, fruit (grapes, berries, bananas), rice cakes, string cheese. These are hard to overeat and most are high-protein.
  • No chips in the house. You reach for what's there. If it's not there, jerky and popcorn fill the same crunch craving at a fraction of the calories.
  • If you DoorDash, you log it. Slip-ups happen. Don't spiral — just track it and eat lighter the next day.

Tracking

  • Weeks 1-3: Log everything in MyFitnessPal (or Cronometer, MacroFactor — whatever you'll actually use). The goal is calibration, not obsession. Learn what 180g protein looks like.
  • Week 4+: Once you can eyeball portions, you can stop logging daily. Weigh yourself 2-3x/week in the morning and track the trend. If the trend stalls for 2+ weeks, log for a few days to find the leak.
  • Protein priority. If you hit nothing else, hit protein. It preserves muscle on a cut, keeps you full, and is the hardest macro to overeat.

Progression

  • Low sets = every set counts. Top sets at RPE 8–9 (2 reps in reserve). If you finish a set feeling like you had 4+ reps left, the weight was too light — the stimulus comes from proximity to failure, not volume.
  • Long rest is a feature, not laziness. 3+ min between working sets on the main compounds. Fully recovered = heavier loads = more growth stimulus per set. Use the rest — don't scroll through it and cut it short.
  • Upper body: push loads. When you hit top of rep range on all 3 sets of the main compound (Incline Press / Pull-up), add 5 lbs next session. This is where DEXA says lean mass needs to grow.
  • Lower body: maintain. Legs are 99th %ile lean. One lower day keeps strength; don't chase PRs. Basketball Saturday covers leg conditioning.
  • Asymmetry fix. DEXA shows 7.1% lean asymmetry in arms (right dominant). On Bulgarian split squats and any single-arm work, do left first, match reps on right. Let the left set the ceiling.
  • Joints over ego. If a joint hurts, drop 20% and build back up. Especially the shoulder — dip → OHP → landmine press is a safe fallback chain.

Cardio

  • Basketball (~2x/week): This is your primary cardio. Don't add to it, don't formalize it. Keep showing up when people need numbers.
  • Optional walks: 20-30 min on rest days. Neighborhood loop if possible. Low-key NEAT increase, not a workout. Do it if you feel like it, skip if you don't.
  • Sauna: Post-workout if you want. Recovery benefit, no meaningful fat loss. Don't count it as a session.

Ground Rules

  1. Delete DoorDash. Reinstalling it requires admitting defeat. The friction is the feature.
  2. Morning gym, non-negotiable window. You have until 7:45am to be at the gym. If it doesn't happen, it moves to afternoon. If afternoon doesn't happen either, it just doesn't happen. No guilt, no makeup.
  3. Eat during the day. If you get to 7pm on 400 calories, the night is going to be a disaster. Front-load.
  4. Protein first, always. Every meal and every snack has a protein component. This is the one macro that actually matters on a cut.
  5. Log or weigh — pick at least one. Either log food in an app or weigh yourself 2-3x/week. Preferably both. You need at least one feedback signal.
  6. No zero weeks. A bad day is fine. Two bad days is fine. But every week needs at least 2 of the 3 gym sessions and 3 days of hitting calories. That's the floor.
  7. Joints over ego. Drop weight on anything that hurts. The shoulder, the knee, the ankle — all of these will try to flare up in the first few weeks as volume increases. Respond by reducing load, not pushing through.
